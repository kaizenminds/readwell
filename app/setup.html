Set up Readwell

About 5 minutes, once. Best done on a laptop. After that you can read on any phone, tablet or computer.

How this works. Readwell has no servers. You make your own copy of its small backend inside your Google account. It reads your Gmail as you, stores your highlights in a Sheet in your Drive, and nobody else, including us, can see any of it.

  1. Copy the backend 1 min

    • Open the template and sign in with the Google account whose Gmail you read newsletters in.
    • Click Overview (the i icon on the left), then Make a copy.
    • Rename the copy to Readwell if you like.
    Open the Readwell template
    Or build it by hand
    • Go to script.google.com and click New project.
    • Replace everything in Code.gs with this code.
    • Next to Services click +, pick Gmail API, keep the name Gmail, click Add.
    • Save with Ctrl+S or Cmd+S.

  2. Run setup 1 min

    • In the function menu at the top, pick setup and click Run.
    • Click Review permissions and choose your account.
    • Google says "Google hasn't verified this app". That is expected: the app is your own copy, not ours. Click Advanced, then Go to Readwell (unsafe), then Allow.
    Setup makes a Sheet in your Drive, three Gmail labels and a daily scan, then looks back over your recent mail for newsletter senders. It never deletes anything.

  3. Deploy it 1 min

    • Click Deploy (top right), then New deployment.
    • Click the gear next to Select type and pick Web app.
    • Execute as: Me. Who has access: Anyone.
    • Click Deploy.
    Why "Anyone"? Is that safe?

    The Readwell app lives on a different website from Google, so it can't use your Google login to reach your backend. "Anyone" lets the app reach it, and your backend then refuses every request that doesn't carry your private 64-character key. Nobody can guess it.

    Treat the connect link like a password: don't post it or send it to anyone. If you think it leaked, run rotateApiKey in your script. The old key stops working at once.

  4. Connect the app 1 min

    • Back in the editor, pick connectLink in the function menu and click Run.
    • The log at the bottom shows your personal connect link. Open it.
    • Readwell opens, connects and shows your inbox.
    To use another device, send that link to yourself (a note or a message to yourself) and open it there. Each device only needs it once.

  5. Pick your newsletters 1 min

    • Open Senders. Setup has listed senders that look like newsletters.
    • Tap Add for the ones you want. Their last 5 days come in straight away, and new issues land every morning.
    • Tap Ignore for the rest. Added senders skip your Gmail inbox from then on.

  6. Put it on your home screen

    • iPhone or iPad: open the connect link in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
    • Android: in Chrome, tap the menu, then Install app.
    • Laptop: in Chrome or Edge, click the install icon in the address bar.
    On iPhone and iPad, add it to the home screen from the connect link itself, so the app opens already connected.

Good to know

Will it delete my mail?

Only if you ask. You can delete an issue yourself, and you can turn on auto-cleanup in Settings, which removes read issues after 5 days unless you saved, tagged, starred or highlighted them. It is off until you switch it on.

When does it check for new issues?

Once a day, at 7 AM in your timezone. Change the time in Settings. Check now runs it straight away.

"Could not reach your backend"

Check the deployment's access is Anyone, and that you used the Web app link ending in /exec. Run connectLink again for a fresh link.

"Your backend refused this key"

The key was changed. Run connectLink and open the new link on each device.

I changed the script. Do I need to redeploy?

Yes: Deploy, Manage deployments, pencil icon, Version: New version, Deploy. The link stays the same.

How do I stop using Readwell?

In your script, Deploy, Manage deployments, Archive. Then delete the project and the "Newsletter Reader Data" Sheet. Your Gmail labels stay, so nothing is lost.
